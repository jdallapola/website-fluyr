PLANS FOR FLUYR WEBSITE

Here's the scaffolding in six stages. Each one is a module with a defined input, output, and decision point, so you can build them incrementally and test in isolation.

**Stage 1. Data acquisition.** Two sources from IBGE: the Censo 2022 "agregados por setor censitário" tabular data (CSV by UF) and the "malha de setores censitários 2022" shapefile. From the tabular side you need rendimento nominal médio mensal do domicílio, distribution of households by income bracket (to compute share above a high threshold), and distribution of households by tipo de espécie (to get casa share versus apartamento share). The polygon side gives you geometry plus the setor code that joins to the tabular data. Decision point at the end of this stage: confirm that Censo 2022 has released the income-by-bracket variables you need at setor level, because early releases sometimes aggregate only at município.

**Stage 2. Data preparation.** Join tabular to geometry on the setor code, filter to urban setores only (tipo = urbano, excluding rural, aglomerado subnormal unless you explicitly want to keep those), drop setores below a minimum number of domicílios to avoid noise in small denominators, and flag setores with suppressed income data (IBGE suppresses when statistical disclosure risk is high, typically in very small tracts). Output is a clean GeoDataFrame with one row per setor and all candidate variables.

**Stage 3. Composite scoring.** Construct the ranking variable. A defensible starting form is the product of standardized z-scores for (share of households above high-income threshold) and (casa share), then rank nationally. Build two or three candidate composites in parallel and inspect the top 100 setores from each on a map to sanity-check: if you recognize Alphaville, Barra da Tijuca condomínios, Lago Sul, Vilaggio Santa Helena, Damha and similar, the metric is working. If you see Itaim and Leblon apartment blocks dominating, the casa filter isn't biting hard enough.

**Stage 4. Geographic aggregation.** Adjacent high-priority setores should be merged into clusters before converting to Google Ads targets. Spatial operation is a dissolve on contiguous selected polygons. For each resulting cluster, compute the centroid and the radius that circumscribes the cluster (or the radius that captures 95% of the internal area if you want to be conservative). Enforce a minimum radius floor, probably 1 km, to stay above Google's minimum targeting size. Output is a table of cluster_id, latitude, longitude, radius, plus descriptive metadata (município, UF, estimated households, mean income).

**Stage 5. Export to Google Ads format.** Transform the cluster table into the Google Ads Editor bulk upload CSV schema for proximity targets. Split into files of 1,000 rows or fewer to respect the bulk upload limit per campaign. Name each target with the cluster_id so conversion data can be joined back. If you want to run CEP-based targeting in parallel as a cross-check, add a stage that maps each cluster to overlapping CEP ranges and produces a second export in postal code format.

**Stage 6. Feedback loop.** Design the conversion tracking upfront, not later. Configure Google Ads conversion tracking with the cluster_id as a custom dimension so you can later join ad performance back to the IBGE-derived clusters. Define a quarterly re-scoring routine that ingests the conversion data, computes cost-per-conversion by cluster, and re-weights the composite metric based on observed performance. After two or three quarters the ranking becomes less dependent on Censo proxies and more dependent on your own revenue signal, which is where the pipeline becomes genuinely defensible.

One cross-cutting note: keep the code modular by stage with intermediate outputs persisted to disk (parquet for tabular, geoparquet or gpkg for spatial), because you'll want to re-run stages 3 through 6 many times while stages 1 and 2 stay stable. Name the Python files in camelCase per your convention: `acquireIbgeData.py`, `prepareSetorData.py`, `scoreSetores.py`, `aggregateClusters.py`, `exportToGoogleAds.py`, `feedbackScoring.py`.
